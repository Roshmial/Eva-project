# Gamma Presentation Delivery

## Procedure

1. Establish the sales logic before generating slides.
   - Extract the literal requested sequence, stakeholders, cases, evidence requirements, and commercial boundaries.
   - Write the causal spine in one sentence before card design: `why now → why the current state is costly, risky, or opaque → what is being proposed → how the methodology builds it → management result and next step`. When selling implementation, include an explicit proposition card before methodology; name the object being implemented and the traceability or control it creates. Treat downstream choices such as placement, vendor, or architecture as supported use cases rather than the main story unless the user explicitly makes that decision central.
   - Treat the user’s stated slide order as a hard constraint. Validate the outline slide by slide before generation.
   - For RSM/TBM offers, position the resource-service model and its financial model as one management contour: RSM describes dependencies and consumption; the financial model evaluates the cost and consequences of scenarios.

2. Build a concise card-by-card brief.
   - Use one slide per distinct job in the narrative; remove semantic duplicates before sending the prompt to Gamma.
   - Separate each card into `VISIBLE TEXT` and `SERVICE — DO NOT DISPLAY`. Keep the visible layer near 40–60 words and at most five elements for a short executive deck; put layout, color, hierarchy, and verification instructions only in the service layer. Keep every mandatory client-facing qualifier in `VISIBLE TEXT`: illustrative status, data provenance, unit definition, acceptance role, residual, and any label needed to interpret a formula. Service notes may tell Gamma how to draw those elements, but they cannot be the only place where their meaning exists. A long methodology or contract specification invites Gamma to shrink text or print production notes instead of designing a slide.
   - For stakeholder-value slides, make each card operational: `Что` (the management result) and `За счёт чего` (the model capability that makes it possible).
   - When the user requests exactly one slide, treat it as the whole deliverable: consolidate the argument into one readable composition, state `ровно 1 слайд` in the generation brief, and verify the export contains one slide.
   - When the user requests one role slide, keep all roles on that one slide and use later slides for decisions or cases, not repeated role benefits.
   - Lock the commercial object before writing cards. If it is a pilot, use one management question, 1–2 services, and 2–3 alternatives consistently across scope, scenario, acceptance, and commercial cards. If it is a full project, remove pilot language completely and show the target operating model, regular cycle, stage gates, transfer, and full-project commercial frame.
   - For a two-slide CEO explanation of a planning-and-actuals model, use two distinct management jobs: slide 1 shows the forward causal chain `catalog and norms → sales plan → resource demand → project economics → company P&L`; slide 2 shows the closed-period boundary and rolling chain `actual consumption → actual direct cost → EAC for open periods → recognized revenue → margin and operating profit`. Keep controls and formulas as compact annotations so the mechanism remains readable at executive distance.
   - Mark client-history figures as historical examples. Mark optimization figures as potential, not a commitment or guaranteed result.
   - In an investment-case deck, lead each application slide with the client situation, decision, and measurable client effect. Put an external case in a compact evidence note; a vendor case supports the effect mechanism and must never become the slide’s protagonist.
   - Express the client effect through measurable fields or formulas such as avoided purchases, lead-time change, released capacity, dual-run duration, RTO/RPO, and expected loss. Use literal client values only when supplied or calculated; otherwise use field names or variables rather than illustrative numbers that can be mistaken for targets.
   - When mirroring a reference case block, preserve its narrative architecture as well as its slide count. For a six-slide block shaped as `summary → detailed cases → synthesis`, do not turn every slide into another methodology step or another independent case.   - For a one-slide infrastructure-value case, lead with incremental value beyond the baseline transformation; omit scale and current-state facts unless they are required to interpret a calculation.
   - Make every value card causal: show how the mechanism forms budget impact and how it removes a Time to Market delay, then tag the resulting money, operational-efficiency, and delivery-speed outcomes with compact markers such as `₽`, `⚙`, and `⏱`.
   - Build value slides in two visual levels: first the two or three client outcomes as large, immediately readable metrics; then the enabling initiatives, each mapped to the budget category and the specific delivery scenario it affects. A slide that opens with mechanisms rather than client outcomes misses the executive job.
   - When a headline saving is decomposed into initiatives, show a quantified contribution only when it comes from client input or a cited benchmark. For the remaining initiatives, name the cost category and calculation base instead of inventing percentages so the total does not become a false allocation.
   - When presenting a duration reduction, state both the absolute and relative estimate against the same denominator. If a seven-day improvement is benchmarked to a two-week window, present it as `до 50% / до 7 дней`; never imply that the full window is the reduction.
- Model Time to Market by delivery scenario, never as a pooled infrastructure total: general standardization applies to typical resource delivery and new-team onboarding; BareMetal applies only to BareMetal issuance; HPC/GPU applies only to queueing and priority access for those scarce resources. Do not add their day estimates together because they describe overlapping or distinct request populations.
- In an infrastructure-value decomposition, make FinOps the primary budget lever and describe its direct Time to Market effect as limited to decision latency; do not use FinOps to inflate delivery-speed claims. Separate a shared automation budget effect from a more specific BareMetal operational effect to avoid double counting.

3. Run grounded research where the deck needs external methodology.
   - Prefer primary sources for TBM, FinOps, cloud architecture, and governance claims.
   - Keep externally sourced frameworks separate from client-provided examples; do not turn a client example into a universal benchmark.

4. Generate or adapt through Gamma deliberately.
   - When the user explicitly requests Gamma, make the editable Gamma document and its URL acceptance criteria; an exported `.pptx` is a verified companion artifact, not a substitute for creating the deck in Gamma.
   - If the requested deliverable is only a Gamma prompt, label it as prompt-ready rather than deck-ready. Scores from outline reviewers validate the brief, not Gamma's actual layout; claim final delivery only after generating, exporting, and inspecting the artifact.
   - Resolve an existing user-provided credential from approved secret storage or session history before declaring that access is unavailable; never expose it in text or saved artifacts.
   - Generate a `presentation` with `cardOptions.dimensions: "16x9"` whenever the user requests a wide-screen deck. If the user says “16:9, but vertical,” keep the 16:9 canvas and build a top-to-bottom reading flow inside each slide and across the storyline; do not switch to portrait dimensions unless explicitly requested.
   - When the user names an existing deck as the design reference, treat the whole visual grammar as a hard requirement: inspect the actual export and preserve cover treatment, grid, card geometry, title hierarchy, density, colors, footer, numbering, and custom-generated visuals. Reusing only `themeId` is insufficient because a Gamma theme does not preserve composition.
   - Lock the title-card contract before generation. For Misha, a true title card contains only the requested title and small subtitle; move the former opening argument to the next slide rather than combining cover and content. Verify the title at full resolution and verify that slide 2 preserves the original first content job.
   - Compare the title slide first. A reference cover shaped as `full-height custom visual on the left + compact title block on the right` must retain that exact composition; a full-width heading, process diagram, stock illustration, or empty image area is a different design and must block delivery.
   - When the user asks for a premium, emotionally attractive deck, use one coherent art direction before generation: large bespoke editorial images occupying roughly 35–55% of a concept slide, controlled negative space, tactile materials and one focal accent. Replace icon rows, clipart, tiny pictograms, robots, brains and decorative neural networks; a small image in a corner does not satisfy the request for full imagery.
   - Ground AI-deck imagery in recognizable business and engineering reality: GPU racks and cooling, model-serving operations, MLOps pipelines, capacity planning, observability screens, product teams and executive decision rooms. Use the deck palette and cinematic lighting to make these scenes premium, but keep abstract ceramic/glass sculptures only as a supporting cover motif; if the subject of an image is not identifiable in two seconds without reading the slide, it is too abstract for a content card.
   - Before generating an image-led deck, write a slide-level visual subject map: `decision job → recognizable real scene → image share → text zone → prohibited clichés`. Do not use one abstract-material prompt across all cards; recurring ceramic arches and glass objects make AI content look decorative rather than credible. Use people only where work or decision-making is the subject, and show plausible screens, equipment and environments without readable fake UI. Use `references/ai-business-visual-direction.md` for the scene map and prompt pattern.
   - If the user says that only parts of the design and copy work, pause further generation and run two slide-by-slide gates first: an art-direction audit (`keep / replace / exact visual / composition`) and an editorial audit (`keep / rewrite / exact visible copy`). Apply one bounded change set to the rejected slides, preserve approved slides literally, then compare untouched text and render every changed slide at full resolution.
   - Remove or rewrite contradictory old design instructions before submitting the prompt. A late “priority” paragraph is less reliable than one conflict-free brief, because Gamma can follow the stale prohibition and produce a visually inconsistent deck.
   - If the new deck intentionally keeps the reference slide count, sequence, or layout jobs, create it with `/v1.0/generations/from-template` from the reference Gamma and map each new slide to the corresponding reference composition. Use a fresh generation with only `themeId` only when the storyline and layout genuinely diverge. Follow `references/gamma-theme-reuse.md` for the decision rule and API recipe.
   - Treat `from-template` as probabilistic rather than exact. If it changes composition or drops a custom visual, stop iterating through Gamma: duplicate the reference PPTX directly, replace text inside the existing shapes, preserve every shape's geometry/type, and generate a new subject-specific visual in the same artistic language for image slots. Verify shape counts and geometry against the reference before delivery.
   - For corrections to an approved deck, adapt the current Gamma with `/v1.0/generations/from-template`; name only the slides to change, state that all others must remain literal, and avoid a fresh storyline generation unless the user requests one.
   - Use a stable theme and an explicit visual instruction: executive B2B, diagrams and cards, no stock people unless requested. Resolve visual instructions before generation: never combine a global prohibition with a per-slide request for the same composition, such as «no circular diagrams» plus «ring process»; choose one compatible form, for example a linear cycle with a return arrow. Ask for editable text, cards, lines, and arrows when information must remain legible; decorative generated images often lose labels and causal structure.
   - For a Russian deck created through the Generate API, use `textMode: "generate"` with `textOptions.language: "ru"` and repeat the Russian-only constraint in the prompt; `preserve` ignores language options and can leave generated labels in English.
   - Prefer a simple matrix or equal cards over a dense hub-and-spoke or long dual-chain diagram when every label is mandatory; Gamma may retain the visual while dropping editable labels.
   - Poll the generation to completion, download the export, and retain the editable Gamma URL plus the exported `.pptx`. For `/generations/from-template`, include `"exportAs": "pptx"` in the request when an immediate export is required; a completed response otherwise may contain the Gamma URL without `exportUrl`.
   - Treat Gamma as the authoring and export system. Use scripts only as quiet QA/orchestration when they materially reduce errors; do not present Python as producing the deck or export. For Misha, prefer direct Gamma work when automation adds no clear value, and do not narrate dependency setup, retries, or other backstage mechanics unless they create a delivery blocker. Report the final Gamma link, verified exports, substantive changes, and QA result.
   - Parse Agent generation completion defensively: current responses may return `gammaId` and `gammaUrl` inside a top-level `gamma` object while `result` contains only the generation summary. Read `completed.gamma.gammaId` / `completed.gamma.gammaUrl` first, then fall back to result fields; otherwise a successful generation can be mistaken for failure and needlessly rerun.
   - Keep no more than two active variants by default: current and one fallback. An explicit user request to preserve or duplicate a version overrides this cleanup rule; create the copy through `/v1.0/generations/from-template`, compare its exported text and slide count with the source, and never delete either preserved version. Before any cleanup, verify the endpoint semantics: `DELETE /v1.0/gammas/{gammaId}` reports deletion, so use it only for rejected drafts, never for the viable fallback; then confirm the current Gamma remains active through the API.

5. Verify the actual generated deck, not the prompt.
   - Run substantive QA before visual QA. From the extracted text, assign one decision job to every slide and read only the slide headlines as a causal story. Check that the deck says why the issue matters, what is proposed, and how the methodology builds it; reject any optional use case that has drifted into the main spine. State separately what the deck proves, what remains illustrative, and what still requires client data or a commercial proposal. A clean render or `PASS` on every slide does not satisfy this gate.
   - Inspect exported slide text and count using `python-pptx`; traverse grouped shapes as well as top-level text frames when checking literal labels.
   - Record word count and minimum font size per slide. For this user, keep ordinary body text at 14 pt or larger where practical; reserve 9–11 pt for sources and explicit footnotes. Split or simplify a slide when Gamma compresses core content below 12 pt.
   - Verify the slide ratio is 16:9, slide count matches the intended narrative, required headings survived, and no duplicate stakeholder slide remains.
   - If the deliverable includes a 12–14-minute talk track, count the actual spoken script rather than extrapolating from slide notes; target roughly 1 300–1 700 words and verify every slide has a corresponding section.
   - Report reviewer scores by criterion and artifact stage. Never present one reviewer’s 10/10 for a single criterion as the overall quality of the deck, and never call a prompt deck-ready before generation and visual inspection.
   - Search extracted text for service-layer labels and production instructions such as `СЛУЖЕБНО`, `НЕ ВЫВОДИТЬ`, layout directions, font sizes, and generation checks. Their presence is a failed deck even when the visible copy is otherwise complete.
   - Compare extracted text for every untouched slide against the prior export after a targeted adaptation; Gamma can silently rewrite a phrase on an unnamed slide. Treat any collateral semantic change as a failed targeted edit: revert it or keep the prior viable version, and archive the prior version only when the named slides changed and all other slides stayed intact.
   - Inspect the actual visual assets or rendered slide for every named visual correction. Text presence does not prove that arrows, labels, spacing, or reading order survived export. Treat a Gamma thumbnail showing a loading placeholder as unverified and inspect the editable page or a rendered PPTX instead.
   - Use `scripts/qa_gamma_export.py` for structural/text gates and `scripts/render_pdf_montage.py` for PDF page renders plus a contact sheet. Screen with the contact sheet, then open every suspected blocker at full slide resolution: montage thumbnails systematically exaggerate small-text problems and cannot justify another edit by themselves.
   - Treat the Gamma agent edit summary as a self-report. Export and inspect even when it says the edit succeeded; conversely, evaluate a warned layout from the render before retrying, because a different composition can still satisfy the slide's job.
   - When Gamma produces a miniature detailed scheme or a dark inset that behaves like a slide inside a slide, replace it with large native cards, a short table or one or two simple chains. The proof element must remain readable at director distance, not merely present in the text layer.
   - When the deck must be Russian, check extracted export text for unintended English headings and captions; regenerate with an explicit Russian-only instruction if any appear.
   - Search the export for every mandatory numeric label and its qualifier. Gamma can create a metric card while omitting the number itself; require figures such as `ДО 15%` as ordinary editable text and verify the literal strings in the PPTX text layer.
   - For an illustrative allocation slide, verify the arithmetic before generation and again against exported literals: pool total, service subtotal, unit cost, other-service amount, visible residual and reconciliation must close exactly. Put calculation-group names and amounts, driver order, and the bridge `unlinked before → allocated by approved rules → residual` in the visible text; service-layer notes do not protect an unlabeled formula in the export. If the compact example mixes TBM Cost Pools, Resource Towers and Solutions, call them «calculation groups» rather than incorrectly presenting them as one taxonomy layer. Use one period for numerator and denominator and a homogeneous service offering or workload class. Keep «Иллюстративный пример; не данные клиента, не benchmark и не достигнутый эффект» adjacent to the figures so Gamma cannot separate the qualification into an unreadable footer.
   - Search for mandatory facts, role wording, commercial boundaries, case labels, and the continuous management cycle. Reject an external deck that still shows square placeholders, unsupported team claims, or a contract CTA before scope, acceptance, team and price are defined. When commercial facts are not yet known, show them as parameters of the next fixed proposal rather than visible blanks. Regenerate with stronger title-level constraints if Gamma compresses or omits a mandatory point.
   - For a continuous-management slide, make the cycle itself the headline when it is mandatory. For operating management use `Планировать → Рассчитывать → Решать → Корректировать`; for a strategy offer use `Стратегия → Реализация дорожной карты → Сопровождение и контроль достижения → Пересмотр стратегии`, and state that value depends on both adaptability and execution support.

## Short diagnostic sales deck pattern

For a maximum-five-slide deck that sells a routed executive diagnosis, use:

1. Promise and target audience.
2. One-pager with `Что / Для кого / Зачем / Что сказать`.
3. Vertical flow `executive entry conversation → one primary track → depth of continuation`, plus only a compact methodology strip.
4. Two clearly labeled example outcomes for distinct tracks, each as `situation → management conclusion → first wave → output`.
5. One further example, the common result card, and a concrete starting step.

Keep named external frameworks as methodological reference points rather than implied endorsements or copied scoring systems. Use official sources, keep long URLs out of the body, and avoid logos unless requested. Mark example findings as illustrative so sales copy cannot be mistaken for achieved client results.

## RSM/TBM Pitch Pattern

For a 12–14-minute RSM/TBM client pitch, adapt the objects and units to infrastructure or AI platform rather than mixing both in one prompt. After value and retained decision rights, show the confirmed commercial object — diagnosis, pilot, or full project — and keep it unchanged through scope, acceptance and CTA.

For a full-project executive deck, preserve this spine: economic decision gap → retained decision rights → management-result screen → decision scenarios → auditable illustrative calculation → service catalog linking business and technical services → demand/resource/cost model → distinct ITFM/TBM/RSM functions → plan/fact/forecast cycle → data readiness → full-project scope → evidence-based team → stage gates → commercial frame. Show what the executive will decide before explaining project composition or methodology. A pilot deck uses a smaller scope and must not inherit full-project promises.

Keep scenarios illustrative unless source, context, calculation basis, and permission to use a historical case are confirmed. Use visibly different compositions for the catalog, mechanism, cost cascade, decision cycle, data-readiness and stage-gate cards. Load the `client-presentation-storylining` skill for the detailed RSM/TBM domain sequence, catalog rules, full-project gates and timing contract.

## Pitfalls

- Do not generate an outline that sells RSM and the financial model as adjacent deliverables; this obscures the causal chain that the customer is buying.
- Do not replace a requested stakeholder-value slide with a list of stakeholder questions; show the concrete management action and the enabling mechanism.
- Do not repeat the stakeholder slide in different wording; the second occurrence consumes attention that should prove decisions and cases.
- Do not trust Gamma to preserve a required concept merely because it appears in the input; its generation can condense content, so assert critical wording through explicit headings and inspect the exported file.
- Do not report a Gamma deck as wide-screen from the generation intent alone; verify the exported `.pptx` ratio.
- Do not accumulate generation experiments as active deliverables; archive superseded variants only after the replacement is verified, otherwise the client cannot identify the current deck.
- Do not present potential savings, selected-scope results, or historical client outcomes as guaranteed results for a new client.
