# Resource and revenue economics

## Resource balance equations

For a human resource in month `m`:

- `demand_hours[m]`: demand generated by plan or actual consumption.
- `capacity_per_person[m]`: productive hours available from one person in that specific month; store it as a monthly driver, not one fixed annual average.
- `required_people[m] = CEILING(demand_hours[m] / capacity_per_person[m], 1)`.
- `available_people[m]`: whole-number staffing input.
- `available_capacity[m] = available_people[m] × capacity_per_person[m]`.
- `deficit_people[m] = MAX(required_people[m] - available_people[m], 0)`.
- `surplus_people[m] = MAX(available_people[m] - required_people[m], 0)`.
- `reserve_hours[m] = MAX(available_capacity[m] - demand_hours[m], 0)`.
- `utilization[m] = demand_hours[m] / available_capacity[m]`; return zero or a clear marker when capacity is zero.

For discrete non-human resources, replace people with whole units and use capacity `1` unless the catalog defines another explicit capacity.

## Cost treatment

For salaried people:

- `person_month_cost[m]`: monthly cost of one person.
- `effective_hourly_cost[m] = person_month_cost[m] / capacity_per_person[m]`.
- `allocated_cost[m] = consumed_hours[m] × effective_hourly_cost[m]`.
- `full_pool_cost[m] = MAX(required_people[m], available_people[m]) × person_month_cost[m]`, unless actual full cost is supplied explicitly.

For licenses and equipment:

- `allocated_cost[m] = consumed_quantity[m] × unit_rate[m]`.

For both:

- `unallocated_pool_cost = MAX(full_pool_cost - allocated_cost, 0)`.
- Project margin excludes idle-capacity cost so individual projects are not penalized for management's staffing choice.
- Direction contribution deducts idle-capacity cost assigned by resource cost center; company profit then deducts remaining company overhead.
- Never multiply a person-month cost by monthly hours when calculating the full pool; hours are used only to derive the allocation rate.

If manually entered project costs exceed the calculated pool, either correct the inputs or provide an explicit full-pool cost that covers them. Never silently allow allocated cost to exceed the pool.

## Boundary scenarios

### Deficit

Input: demand 200 hours, capacity 168 hours/person, availability 1 person.

Expected:

- required people: 2;
- deficit: 1;
- surplus: 0;
- utilization: about 119%.

### Whole-person surplus

Input: demand 100 hours, capacity 168 hours/person, availability 2 people.

Expected:

- required people: 1;
- surplus: 1;
- reserve: 236 hours;
- utilization: about 29.8%;
- reserve cost remains in the unallocated pool and reduces company profit.

### Plan-demand convolution

For a project with a 33.6-hour resource norm and a three-month profile of one third each, one project starting in January must generate 11.2 hours in January, February and March. Test both the detail layer and the final resource aggregate.

## Revenue and hour economics

For a rolling project view:

- `recognized_actual_to_date + forecast_recognition = contract_amount`.
- Build a monthly rolling-cost profile from closed actual months plus open forecast months.
- Allocate full contract revenue across that profile; actual-to-date recognition is the closed-month share and forecast recognition is the remainder.
- Costs may be below or above contract revenue. They change timing weights, margin and per-hour economics, not total contract revenue.
- For the plan, calculate recognized revenue by product and sale cohort first, normalize the visible timing profile to the full sale amount, then aggregate to directions.
- For a costless product, use an explicit sale-month recognition rule instead of dividing by zero.
- `planned_revenue_per_hour = contract_amount / sold_hours`.
- `actual_recognized_revenue_per_hour = recognized_actual_to_date / actual_hours`.
- `margin_per_actual_hour = (recognized_actual_to_date - actual_direct_cost) / actual_hours`.
- `margin_percentage = (recognized_actual_to_date - actual_direct_cost) / recognized_actual_to_date`.

When actual hours rise while contract value remains fixed, revenue per actual hour must fall. When those extra hours also increase cost, margin per hour and margin percentage must fall as well.

## Dimensional collision checks

- Give every calculation row an explicit measure identity; `scenario + resource_id` is not a unique lookup key when a sheet contains rate, availability, capacity and cost for the same resource.
- Build plan money on the visible `product × resource × month` detail layer, then aggregate prepared cost by direction.
- Verify a discrete-resource case with exact arithmetic. If quantity `37` and unit rate `0.7` do not produce `25.9`, inspect lookup criteria before changing business assumptions.
- Perturb availability while holding quantity and rate fixed; direct cost must remain unchanged. Perturb the rate while holding quantity and availability fixed; resource demand must remain unchanged.
