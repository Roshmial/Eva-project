# Expense Review

## Purpose

Help the user improve personal financial efficiency by reviewing current spending, identifying recurring leakage, and proposing practical expense optimization.

The goal is not blind cost-cutting, but better allocation of money in support of the user's larger financial goals, including faster mortgage closure.

## When to Use

Use this skill for:
- monthly expense reviews;
- spending pattern analysis;
- identifying unnecessary recurring costs;
- prioritizing what to reduce, keep, renegotiate, or eliminate;
- planning a more efficient personal spending structure.

## Scope

Analyze spending through the following categories:
- mandatory fixed costs;
- important quality-of-life costs;
- optional lifestyle costs;
- impulsive or weak-value costs;
- hidden recurring leakage.

## Procedure

1. Clarify the time period and available data:
   - month,
   - quarter,
   - rough estimate,
   - category-level summary,
   - transaction-level details.
2. Classify expenses into:
   - essential,
   - useful,
   - optional,
   - impulsive,
   - unclear.
3. Identify:
   - recurring leaks,
   - bloated categories,
   - low-value spending,
   - subscriptions and passive drains,
   - costs that can be renegotiated or replaced.
4. Evaluate optimization ideas by:
   - money saved,
   - implementation difficulty,
   - effect on quality of life,
   - sustainability.
5. Produce the answer in this structure:
   - short conclusion,
   - key leakage areas,
   - recommended cuts or changes,
   - what should not be cut,
   - estimated impact,
   - next step.

## Rules

- Do not moralize or shame the user.
- Do not recommend extreme austerity unless explicitly requested.
- Protect expenses that sustain health, work capacity, or strategic quality of life.
- Prefer recurring structural savings over one-off symbolic cuts.
- If mortgage acceleration is relevant, explicitly connect savings opportunities to that goal.

## Pitfalls

- Do not treat all discretionary expenses as bad.
- Do not optimize small expenses while ignoring large structural ones.
- Do not assume the cheapest option is the best option.
- Do not ignore fatigue, convenience, or work-performance effects.

## Verification

Check that:
- spending is grouped clearly;
- leakage is specific, not vague;
- recommendations are realistic;
- the output includes both savings potential and trade-offs;
- the next step is concrete.
